01  /  PROBLEM STATEMENT
The WAL Durability Dilemma
What is the WAL?
A sequential on-disk log that records every write before it reaches the in-memory MemTable. In RocksDB's LSM-Tree, it is the sole durability guarantee — without it, any crash discards all uncompacted data.
The Core Tension
Every WAL design choice trades off safety against speed. Enabling fsync() guarantees crash durability but collapses throughput by ~300×. These tradeoffs are buried in C++ internals with no external documentation of their quantitative cost.
Research Question
Can we instrument the RocksDB source to quantify the exact performance cost of each durability decision, and identify which recovery mode minimises MTTR?

01  /  CONTEXT
LSM-Tree Architecture  —  Where WAL Fits
Key Concepts
⚛
Atomicity
A Put() either fully persists (WAL + MemTable) or doesn't appear after recovery. Partial writes are detected via CRC-32C checksums.
🔒
Durability
fsync() flushes the kernel buffer to physical media. Without it, a power cut silently discards everything in OS page cache.
🔄
Recovery
On restart, RocksDB replays the WAL sequentially to rebuild the MemTable. Speed depends on the WALRecoveryMode enum.
